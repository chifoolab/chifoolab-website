Shaping the food education system for children together

The Children's Food Education Lab is a social laboratory working to transform Switzerland's fragmented food education system for children into a more holistic and effective one.

Why act together

What we eat has consequences far beyond the plate

What we eat has consequences for our health, our environment and our economy. Children are a particularly vulnerable group — and childhood is a critical phase for developing sustainable food skills.

~25% of the Swiss population's ecological footprint comes from food consumption1
CHF 17 bn in diet-related health costs in Switzerland in 20202
13% of 6- to 17-year-olds are overweight or obese3

1 Federal Office for the Environment (FOEN). (2022). Umwelt Schweiz 2022. Bericht des Bundesrates. FOEN. www.bafu.admin.ch/de/ub22

2 De Luca, K. & Muller, A. (2025). Hidden costs of the Swiss agrifood system: Case study for the State of Food and Agriculture Report of the FAO – SOFA 2024. Research Institute of Organic Agriculture (FiBL), Frick. Commissioned by the Federal Office for Agriculture (FOAG) and the FAO.

3 Federal Food Safety and Veterinary Office (FSVO). (2025). Nationale Ernährungserhebung bei Kindern und Jugendlichen – menuCH-Kids Report. Bern: FSVO.

What is food education

A holistic approach that lasts a lifetime

Food education is a holistic approach that empowers children to understand food in its many dimensions — health, ecological, social, cultural and economic — and, on that basis, to make independent, responsible and reflective decisions. Through discovering, tasting, growing and cooking, children build skills and develop a positive relationship with food. This process continues throughout life and is embedded in socio-cultural everyday life. Responsibility for food education is shared across different people and places — family, educators, health professionals, supermarkets, media and many others.

Action fields

Eight action fields for systemic change

The Lab has identified eight thematic action fields where we are jointly focusing our efforts — to drive systemic change in today's food education system for children.

Look around before acting

Understand the system as a whole and seek exchange with existing initiatives.

Strengthen families' food skills

Expand advisory services and seek dialogue with marginalised families.

Learn from and with one another

Share resources and educational offers, build bridges between cultures and languages.

Build a shared understanding

Develop a national competency framework for all stages of life.

Leverage local synergies

Coordinate initiatives and build local dialogue.

Communicate more effectively

Spread reliable information and reach the right audiences.

Advance the topic in politics

Advocate for food education, regulate advertising and supply.

Anchor it structurally

Embed food education in school curricula as well as in initial and continuing education.
