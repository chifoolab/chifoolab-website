The Compass

The Compass provides orientation in the food education system for children

The Compass brings together the insights and ideas of the Children's Food Education Lab. It consists of eight thematic action fields that invite reflection and shared action.

Action fields and audio contributions

Each action field is complemented by an audio contribution that explains its significance and places it in a broader context.

Look around before acting
＋

Approaches

  • Before starting new projects, seek exchange with existing initiatives to leverage synergies and draw on existing experience.
  • Involve relevant target groups and stakeholders in developing activities to make them more needs-based and effective.
  • Bundle initiatives and implement coordinated activities for different age groups to ensure coherence.
Strengthen families' food skills
＋

Approaches

  • Make existing advisory services for families on nutrition more visible and accessible in several languages.
  • Extend advisory services for families beyond early childhood.
  • Raise families' awareness of nutrition and refer them to suitable services during paediatric check-ups.
  • Support families in navigating social media content about food.
  • Seek dialogue with marginalised families to better understand their needs and develop targeted measures.
  • Avoid using food as a reward or distraction.
  • Offer and subsidise holiday camps for children that combine gardening, cooking and food discovery.
  • Raise influencers' awareness of their impact on children's eating behaviour through social media.
Learn from and with one another
＋

Approaches

  • Build a strong community through regular exchange between stakeholders.
  • Invite stakeholders to a nationwide transdisciplinary forum to initiate a national food education agenda.
  • Explore new ways to build bridges between cultures and languages.
  • Bundle and make food education programmes and teacher training accessible across cantons.
Build a shared understanding
＋

Approaches

  • Develop a national competency framework for food education and the food environment across all stages of life, based on scientific evidence and expert knowledge.
  • Jointly define the roles and responsibilities of individual stakeholders — such as families, schools, canteens, sports clubs, etc.
  • Develop binding guidelines for catering at public events attended by children (e.g. sports camps, festivals).
Leverage local synergies
＋

Approaches

  • Set up local and cantonal coordination to bundle initiatives, make better use of synergies and unlock new opportunities.
  • Open school kitchens to associations, for example, to run cooking workshops with families.
  • Give schoolchildren insight into food businesses, processing and distribution.
  • Build collaboration between school management, teachers, municipal representatives, families and chefs to holistically strengthen food education as part of school culture.
Communicate more effectively
＋

Approaches

  • Share and promote the visibility of good practices in food education.
  • Reach the relevant target group with your own initiative.
  • Spread reliable information about food and nutrition in public media.
  • Design a professional, creative and innovative communication plan and secure the support needed for it.
Advance the topic in politics
＋

Approaches

  • Identify decision-makers within your own action field.
  • Build partnerships with public health organisations.
  • Develop preventive and economic arguments about the cost-benefit ratio of food education.
  • Reduce highly processed foods in school canteens.
  • Reduce sugar, salt and fat content in foods aimed at children.
  • Ban food marketing and advertising aimed at children.
  • Regulate food advertising on social media aimed at children.
Anchor it structurally
＋

Approaches

  • Schedule dedicated food education lessons in the primary school timetable.
  • Use existing subjects (e.g. maths, language) for a cross-curricular approach to build food education skills.
  • Use food as a topic for everyday education for sustainable development (ESD).
  • Give children access to gardening at every daycare centre and school.
  • Include food education in the initial and continuing training of teachers, educators, canteen chefs and staff, social workers, childcare workers, health professionals, etc.
  • Provide stronger support for food education in institutions that regularly accompany children in daily life — especially daycare centres, kindergartens and playgroups.
